Counting What You Say

A small Python script for finding the most frequent words in any text — and what it reveals when you run it on your own writing.
February 25, 2026 · 2 min · 222 words · Tyler
Table of Contents
  • The Script
  • Running It
  • What It Reveals

Every writer has verbal tics — words they reach for too often without noticing. Mine is “just.” I used to scatter it across emails and documents like punctuation, softening every sentence until nothing felt certain.

I wrote this script to hold myself accountable.

The Script#

It reads from standard input, strips punctuation, lowercases everything, and returns the top ten words by frequency. Nothing clever — just Counter doing its job.

count.py

Running It#

Pass any text file through stdin:

run.sh
$ ./run.sh my-essay.txt
Word                  Count
----------------------------
the                      47
and                      31
a                        28
just                     19   ← there it is
...

What It Reveals#

The top handful of results are always the usual suspects — “the”, “and”, “a” — and you can ignore those. What’s interesting is the first content word to appear. That word is doing a lot of work in your text, whether you meant it to or not.

For me, running this on a month of writing surfaced three things I overuse: “just”, “really”, and “kind of.” All hedges. All ways of making a statement smaller before anyone can push back on it.

The script doesn’t fix anything. That part is still up to you. But naming a habit is the first step toward changing it — which is true whether you’re debugging code or trying to write with more conviction.
